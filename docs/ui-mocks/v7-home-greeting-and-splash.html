Home greeting, doodles and the written splash

Three proposals for Voxa on Android, built on the v6 tokens. The green "Ready to dictate" goes; the greeting becomes the header, drawn over by a small ink doodle that changes with the time of day, your own milestones and a few occasions. The splash becomes the nib writing the word Voxa. Section three brings the greeting, the doodle and the splash to the desktop app. Section four checks every colour pair against WCAG and fixes the ones that fail.

1. Home: a big greeting with a doodle

The greeting is two words and your name, set at 40: on one line when it fits, otherwise the name moves to its own line. Above it, a 112 dp band where a sepia pen line draws the horizon and then the doodle, all finished by 1.4 seconds, once per doodle per day. The avatar stays in the top right; every doodle keeps that corner clear. When nothing is wrong, nothing is said: the screen is the greeting and your numbers.

Every doodle

One pen, one sepia ink, a 2 dp round stroke on a 1.5 dp horizon, and a 12 % wash for anything that glows. Five follow the time of day, one marks the weekend, four come from your own use (the streak tally is drawn from the real count, shown here at 7 and 30), and two are occasions everyone has: your birthday and New Year. Nothing is tied to a country, a religion or a region. Each is 4 to 40 short paths in a 343 by 112 box, 0.5 to 1.5 KB as path data. The captions come from the same time table as the greetings.

The same doodle on different days

So a daily user does not see one identical drawing for months, each time of day doodle varies a little with the date: how many birds and where, which cloud and where, which of four star layouts. Every position comes from a short list of safe spots, never a free random value, and the date is the seed, so a day's drawing is the same all day. At night the moon is the real phase for the date, worked out from the length of a lunar month, not always a crescent.

When something is wrong

A single 48 dp notice sits under the greeting, only while there is a problem, and the whole row is the button. If two things are wrong, it shows the one that blocks dictation first (signed out, then microphone, then keyboard, then offline) and says "and 1 more". It never shows for a healthy state. While it shows, the doodle is drawn already finished with no flourish, so nothing moves next to the problem.

Your birthday, in Settings

The only occasion that needs anything from you, so it is optional and off until you add it. Day and month only, never the year, kept on this phone. On the day it wins over everything else on Home.

The greeting pool

Picked once when Home opens, by the first rule that matches, top to bottom: birthday, then New Year, then a milestone, then the day of the week, then the time of day. Within a rule with more than one line it rotates by date.

Doodle motion and build

  • Beat 1, horizon: 0 to 360 ms, the line draws left to right, FastOutSlowIn.
  • Beat 2, subject: strokes start at 240 ms, 70 to 120 ms apart, each 110 to 520 ms, CubicBezier(0.4, 0, 0.2, 1). Everything is drawn by about 1,300 ms.
  • Beat 3, wash: fills fade 0 to 1 over 240 ms as the last stroke lands.
  • One short flourish: 1,000 to 1,400 ms, once: a star twinkles, the candle flickers, steam rises 4 dp, the rays turn. Every doodle is finished by 1,400 ms (each caption above says when, computed from its parts).
  • When it plays: once per doodle per calendar day, the first time Home shows it. After that, and when coming back from another tab or after the app was closed by the system, Home shows the finished drawing. The doodle is picked only when Home opens.
  • With a problem notice: drawn finished, no flourish.
  • Reduced motion (system animator scale 0 or Remove animations on): the finished drawing, no movement at all.

Compose

  • Each doodle is a list of Stroke(path, startMs, durMs) built once with PathParser().parsePathString(d).toPath() inside remember.
  • One Animatable(0f..1100f) drives the clock. In Canvas, each stroke uses PathMeasure.getSegment(0, len * p, dst) and drawPath(dst, ink, style = Stroke(2.dp, cap = Round)).
  • Colours come from vx.accent, so dark mode is free. No images, no Lottie, about 150 lines for the engine plus the path strings.
  • No date tables: New Year is fixed, the birthday is yours, milestones come from your own stats. The daily variation is a pure function of the date (a 32 bit hash seeds a tiny generator), so the same cases can be tested on Android and desktop.

2. Splash: the pen writes Voxa

The nib from the new icon writes the wordmark as one continuous monoline stroke, finishing with a swash that comes back under the word and crosses the x, the way you would cross a t. The nib keeps a writer's slant (35 degrees) and sways only a few degrees with the stroke direction, the way a held pen does; it never spins to follow the line. Then it lifts away, the ink settles, and one line of description rises in. The owner chose variant B (below): the nib drifts in unhurried, rests on the paper, and starts the V slowly. 2,420 ms in total, then Home fades in over 300 ms. A tap anywhere skips it.

Dark mode

The background becomes the icon's own tile colour, #211B17, the ink a warm off white #F2EDE8, the nib the icon's light sepia #D99366, the description 74 % off white (9.8 to 1). The Android 12 system splash already shows the icon on that tile, so the hand off is seamless.

Hand off from the system splash

Android 12 and later always shows the launcher icon first. Set windowSplashScreenBackground to the splash colour and the animated icon to the bare nib. Our Compose sequence begins with the nib already on screen, so beat 1 is the nib travelling from the centre to the start of the V rather than appearing from nothing.

Reduced motion

The finished frame (wordmark and description, no nib) shows for 400 ms and Home crossfades in. Cold start only; a warm start goes straight to Home.

Compose

One Path from the SVG string below, one PathMeasure. Each frame: getSegment(0, len * p) for the ink, getPosition(len * p) and getTangent for the nib, which is a 10 line Path drawn with withTransform { translate; rotate }.

Variant B is chosen; variant A is kept for reference

A was the first timing. B, the one the owner chose, keeps the same write and the same ending, but takes its time getting started: the nib fades and drifts in over 450 ms instead of 250, rests on the paper for 120 ms (a small press, and the first drop of ink appears under it), then starts the V slowly and finds its pace. The ending is untouched, so B is A plus 420 ms, all of it in the first half second. Both loop from the same start here, so you can watch them side by side.

3. Desktop: the greeting, the doodle and the splash

The same three ideas in the Windows and Mac app, on the v7 Fluent window. Home's header becomes the doodle band with the big two word greeting under it. The "Ready to dictate" line goes: when nothing is wrong the window says nothing about readiness, and an InfoBar appears only while something needs you. The splash plays inside the window's own content area, only the first time you open Home after Voxa starts.

Desktop splash

When you open the Home window for the first time since Voxa started, the content area is blank paper and the nib writes Voxa in its centre, at 1.25 times the phone size, with exactly the phone timing, variant B (2,420 ms). Then Home crossfades in over 300 ms and the doodle draws itself. The title bar stays a normal title bar the whole time, so it never reads as a separate splash window.

When the desktop splash plays

  • Only on a cold open of Home: the first time you open the window after Voxa starts, from the tray icon, the tray menu, or launching Voxa again from Start or the Dock. Later opens in the same run go straight to Home.
  • Never at login. Start with Windows (or a Mac login item) starts Voxa in the tray with no window, so there is nothing to play.
  • Never over other apps. It is drawn inside Voxa's own window, not a separate topmost window. It never opens a window, raises one or takes focus by itself.
  • Only when you are looking. If the window opens without becoming the active window (Windows refuses the focus and flashes the taskbar instead), or it opens straight to a page to fix something (Sign in from the strip, a notification), it skips to that page.
  • Skippable: any click or key goes straight to Home, with the same 300 ms crossfade.
  • Reduced motion (Windows: Animation effects off; Mac: Reduce motion): the finished wordmark, still, no nib, for 400 ms, then Home crossfades in. The doodle shows finished.

Building it in GPUI 0.2, cheaply

  • One canvas, two paths. A canvas() element paints with window.paint_path. The wordmark string has only M and C commands, so a 30 line parser turns it into a PathBuilder once.
  • Stroke reveal by length: GPUI's PathBuilder::dash_array(&[px(len * p), px(len)]) already measures the path and keeps only the first len * p, the same trick as the SVG here. Round caps and joins go through with_style(PathStyle::Stroke(StrokeOptions::default().with_line_cap(Round)...)).
  • The nib needs the point and tangent at len * p: lyon::algorithms::measure::PathMeasurements, built once. lyon 1.0 is already a GPUI dependency, so naming it in Cargo.toml adds no build weight. The nib's four small shapes are rebuilt each frame with PathBuilder::rotate and translate.
  • Clock: window.request_animation_frame() while the splash runs (2.42 s plus the 300 ms crossfade), then it stops asking, so idle cost is zero. Home's root takes .opacity(x) for the crossfade.
  • Doodles: the same engine. GPUI cannot clip a path to a shape, so the hatch lines are stored already clipped (a few line segments per doodle, computed once from this page).
  • Cost: one stroked path of about 250 dp and five tiny fills per frame, tessellated in well under a millisecond; no images, no fonts, no new crates.

4. Colour contrast, light and dark

Every text and component pair the app and keyboard actually use, computed here in the page with the WCAG 2.x relative luminance formula (alpha colours are flattened onto their real background first). Targets: text under 18 pt needs 4.5 to 1 for AA and 7 to 1 for AAA; large text 3 and 4.5; icons and component edges 3 to 1 (AA, there is no AAA for those). Body text (100 % and 72 %) reaches AAA in both themes.
